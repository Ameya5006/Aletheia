# Healthcare dataset audit — UCI 296

Audit date: 2026-10-05. Selected for an **educational, non-clinical** discharge-time readmission-risk audit. Primary records: [UCI dataset 296](https://archive.ics.uci.edu/dataset/296/diabetes+130-us+hospitals+for+years+1999-2008), DOI [10.24432/C5230J](https://doi.org/10.24432/C5230J), CC BY 4.0; associated [Strack et al. 2014 paper](https://doi.org/10.1155/2014/781670). Attribution: Strack, DeShazo, Gennings, Olmo, Lucas, 2014, *Impact of HbA1c Measurement on Hospital Readmission Rates*. The paper analyzes a narrower ~70,000 patient-record study cohort; its statistics must not be confused with the UCI archive's encounter counts.

## Source and file evidence

Official HTTPS archive `https://archive.ics.uci.edu/static/public/296/diabetes+130-us+hospitals+for+years+1999-2008.zip`: 3,170,254 bytes, SHA-256 `f82ac129da2ddd2299391ff6fbae3a6a58b3edcf59ac9d7bd480c00fe453112a`. Members: `diabetic_data.csv`, 19,159,383 bytes, SHA-256 `0689e7ec031237dc63031b938805c48377748761a3b26acab621567afa24df97`; `IDS_mapping.csv`, 2,547 bytes, SHA-256 `f1bb82b471cb34649352597572c9b1fb00bd27f77b9f5a22a03dc3eb1039749e`. These are pinned byte identities observed from the official archive, not a publisher-signed checksum. Retrieval used Windows `curl.exe` with TLS verification. Python `urllib` on this machine rejected an expired certificate chain; the foundation's downloader fails closed if the local TLS stack has the same problem.

Raw CSV: 101,766 rows, 50 ordered columns. Unit: one inpatient encounter, 1999–2008, across 130 US hospitals/networks, with a diabetes diagnosis. `encounter_id` is unique in all 101,766 rows; `patient_nbr` has 71,518 unique values and 30,248 additional encounters. No duplicate complete rows or predictor rows after dropping encounter ID and outcome were observed. There is no usable encounter date, hospital identifier, or current-day clinical validation set.

Raw `readmitted` counts: `<30` 11,357; `>30` 35,545; `NO` 54,864. Paper semantics define early readmission as within 30 days; `>30` and `NO` are both binary negatives, while their raw distinction is retained for audit. Positive prevalence is 11.16% in the raw file. This is an observed historical administrative outcome, not preventability or causal effect.

At **discharge**, exclude dispositions 11 (expired), 13/14 (hospice), and 19/20/21 (expired variants). Observed excluded rows: 2,423 (11: 1,642; 13: 399; 14: 372; 19: 8; 20: 2; 21: 0). Eligible cohort: 99,343 encounters, 69,990 patients; 11,314 positives, 35,502 `>30`, 52,527 `NO`. The 30-day outcome is not known until follow-up. This exclusion is valid only for the discharge-time decision and changes the population being studied. The paper also restricts some analyses to a first encounter per patient; Aletheia retains repeated encounters and groups them rather than claiming its cohort equals the paper's.

## Field dictionary and feature decision

The complete ordered 50-field list and exact one-role assignment live in `configs/datasets/healthcare_uci296_v1.toml` and `configs/features/healthcare_uci296_v1.toml`. Every field has exactly one role. Prediction (13): admission type/source codes; time in hospital; counts of labs, procedures, medications, prior outpatient/emergency/inpatient contacts and diagnoses; maximum glucose result, A1C result, diabetes-medication indicator. These are encounter or prior-history observations that should be available by discharge, subject to future operational timestamp verification. Numeric codes for admission type/source are categorical, not quantities. The eight count/stay fields are quantitative. The result and indicator fields are categorical.

Audit-only: race, gender, age band. `race` has 2,273 `?` values; gender contains three `Unknown/Invalid` records, and age is binned. These attributes enable **descriptive conditional subgroup analysis**, not a claim of bias absence. Identifiers/metadata: `encounter_id`, `patient_nbr`, derived `row_key = uci296-<encounter_id>`; none enters predictors. Raw/derived targets: `readmitted`, `readmitted_30d`.

Excluded: weight (98,569 `?`, 96.86%); payer (40,256 `?`); medical specialty (49,949 `?`); diagnosis codes 1/2/3 (21/358/1,423 `?` and 717/749/790 observed categories); detailed medications and `change` (temporal/actionability complexity and rare levels); discharge disposition (death/hospice/transfer and outcome adjacency). Exclusion is conservative, not a claim that each field is intrinsically unusable. Admission type/source have 8/17 categories, medical specialty 73, payer 18, and several diagnosis values appear once. Mapping CSV defines administrative codes; `NULL`, `Not Available`, `Not Mapped`, and `Unknown/Invalid` descriptions are semantic unknowns, not automatically numeric missing. No data-driven imputation is fitted in this milestone. Exact `?` becomes missing only in seven documented fields; unknown labels remain visible. Training-stage imputation/encoding must be fit within group CV folds.

## Split and feasibility

`patient_nbr` is the group key. SHA-256 of `42:<patient_nbr>` assigns each patient to the 20% hash interval or the training complement, independent of outcomes; this prioritizes stable isolation over exact stratification. The lock binds raw SHA-256, feature and target versions, method/seed, partition/class counts, and canonical patient-membership SHA-256. Observed train: 79,808 encounters, 56,178 patients, class 0 70,734, class 1 9,074. Held out: 19,535 encounters, 13,812 patients, class 0 17,295, class 1 2,240. Total coverage: 99,343 encounters/69,990 patients. Positive rates 11.37%/11.47%; approximately 80/20 by encounter. No patient or encounter crosses the boundary. Training CV must also group by patient. This is a random patient split, not temporal or hospital external validation.

The CSV is ~19 MB; an all-string pandas inspection occupied ~263 MB, feasible on an 8 GB Windows laptop. High-cardinality diagnoses and rare medications motivate the narrow initial feature set and fold-local preprocessing. Fairness comparisons are possible for observed race/gender/age groups, but small/unknown groups and missing race restrict conclusions; no fairness metric has been computed. Counterfactuals may only modify feasible discharge-time variables under a later clinical review; most historical counts, identity, demographics, diagnosis and administered care are not actionable. Similar-case retrieval is feasible on training cases with a versioned distance metric, privacy controls and patient exclusion; no retrieval system exists yet. Historical care, coding and readmission practices, missing external validation, possible unobserved hospital effects, target ascertainment and survivorship/cohort selection rule out clinical deployment.

Alternative: MIMIC-style EHR data could offer timestamps and richer clinical validity but adds access, compute, privacy and governance costs. The existing credit set is small and well controlled but cannot demonstrate healthcare. UCI 296 supports the educational audit with explicit limitations. No model performance has been measured on this dataset.
